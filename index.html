CrunchSim

A simulator of how industry crunches stuff into bits, played as a plant-building score game. Grinding earns credits into your bank. The bank buys machines and upgrades. Your score is your net worth.

  1. Feed. Choose a preset or dial in your own mix of steel, cast iron, aluminum, copper, brass, pot metal, wood, rubber, plastic, glass, granite, limestone, hydrogel and water. Click a material name for its properties.
  2. Flowsheet. Add machines in order. Each one takes its input from the head feed or from an output port of an earlier machine. Separators have two output ports. Click a node to see it in the cam and edit its settings.
  3. Run. The batch is processed at the rate the slowest machine allows. The mission clock, power, nitrogen, wear and product value are tracked live. What the products sell for, minus feed, power and consumables, lands in your bank when the batch completes.
  4. Contracts. Clients post jobs with a spec sheet: purity, recovery, a size window and an energy cap. They supply the feed. Bins that meet the spec ship; stars decide the fee. Every contract has an obvious machine that is the wrong one.
  5. Upgrade. You start with a hammermill shredder and a few thousand dollars. Unsorted shred sells at a discount, so the bank panel's NEXT PURCHASE block ranks the sorters that would add the most margin to the end of your line; buy them one at a time. Buy other machines from the flowsheet panel, raise a machine type's level for more capacity and efficiency, buy plant upgrades for bigger batches and cheaper power, and sign supplier contracts to unlock richer feeds. Net worth is bank plus everything you own, and it sets your rank.

The science in the numbers

  • P80 is the size that 80 % of the mass passes. The reduction ratio is feed P80 over product P80.
  • Specific energy follows Bond's law: E = 10 · Wi · (1/√P80 − 1/√F80) kWh/t with sizes in micrometres, divided by machine efficiency and by how well the machine's mechanism (compression, impact, shear, attrition, cutting, hydraulic shear) actually breaks that material.
  • Brittle things (rock, glass, cast iron, zinc) fracture under compression and impact. Ductile metals bend and ball up: they need shear or cutting. Fibrous wood is cut or hammered. Elastomers bounce unless cooled below their glass transition. Gels are sheared through micron gaps. Liquids cannot be crushed at all: freeze them, or atomize them.
  • Separators sort instead of breaking: magnets take ferrous, eddy currents fling conductors, air lifts light fluff, screens size, and a heavy liquid floats aluminum off zinc, brass and copper.
